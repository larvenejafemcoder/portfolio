About me

First-year Software Engineering student at HUFLIT with a broad programming foundation and a strong interest in understanding how software works beyond simply using frameworks.

I currently work with C++, C#, Python, JavaScript, and TypeScript, and have experience experimenting with web development, development tooling, Linux environments, and personal software projects. My strongest trait is not mastery of a particular framework, but the ability to teach myself unfamiliar technologies, troubleshoot problems, and keep digging until I understand what is actually happening.

I enjoy building things as a way of learning and tend to explore multiple areas of computing rather than staying inside one technology stack. My current interests include software engineering, systems, Linux, open source, developer tooling, computer architecture, cybersecurity, and web development.

I am currently focused on turning my existing self-taught programming experience into stronger engineering fundamentals through university study, personal projects, collaboration, and eventually professional experience.

Strengths

  • Independent Learning

    A major part of my programming background comes from self-directed learning. I am comfortable starting with limited knowledge, reading documentation, experimenting, breaking things, and gradually figuring out how a technology works.

  • Debugging & Troubleshooting

    I enjoy investigating problems rather than immediately looking for a workaround. When something breaks, I look at the underlying environment, dependencies, configuration, drivers, tooling, or implementation instead of treating the error message as the entire problem.

  • Technical Curiosity

    My interests span multiple layers of computing, from web development and programming languages to Linux, computer architecture, operating systems, and cybersecurity. This gives me a tendency to ask "why does this work?" rather than only "how do I make this work?"

  • Adaptability

    I have experience moving between different languages, operating systems, editors, tools, and development environments. I am comfortable being a beginner in something new and learning as I go.

  • Project-Based Learning

    I learn most effectively by building. Personal projects, experiments, and technical side projects have been an important part of developing my programming skills.
